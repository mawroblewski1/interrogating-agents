Mirroring is the technique of reflecting back a word, phrase, or the essential meaning of what the suspect just said, signaling that you heard them and inviting elaboration — without asking a direct question.

**How it works:**
In conversation, we are often composing our next response while the other person is still speaking. Mirroring breaks this pattern. By returning the suspect's own words to them, you signal that you were genuinely listening, and you create a natural prompt for them to continue without the pressure of a direct question.

**Three forms of mirroring:**

1. **Word mirroring** — repeat the last 1-3 words with a slightly rising intonation
   - Suspect: "The thing that worries me is that this is irreversible."
   - Interrogator: "Irreversible." [pause]
   - Effect: The suspect elaborates on what irreversibility means to them.

2. **Phrase mirroring** — reflect the most emotionally loaded or distinctive phrase
   - Suspect: "It's not about the policy itself — it's about who gets to decide."
   - Interrogator: "Who gets to decide."
   - Effect: The suspect unpacks the democratic concern beneath their stated position.

3. **Meaning mirroring** (complex reflection) — paraphrase the core meaning
   - "So the question for you isn't whether this is a good idea — it's whether this is the right process."

**Timing:** Use mirroring immediately after the suspect has said something worth developing. A well-placed mirror can produce 2-3 turns of unprompted elaboration that a direct question would not.

**Combine with silence:** After mirroring, do not immediately follow up. The silence that follows a mirror is productive — let the suspect fill it.
