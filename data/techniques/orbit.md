ORBIT is a structured rapport-building model used in investigative interviewing contexts. It describes five conditions that need to be established for a suspect to feel safe enough to engage genuinely.

**O — Open up**
Create the conditions for the suspect to speak freely. Signal that this is a genuine conversation, not an interrogation. Use a relaxed tone, non-threatening body language (or equivalent conversational signals), and avoid evaluative language early on.

**R — Reinforce**
Acknowledge the suspect's contributions specifically and genuinely. Not hollow praise, but specific recognition: "That distinction you made between X and Y — I hadn't heard it put that way before."

**B — Build**
Build on what the suspect shares. Follow their lead, develop the threads they introduce, and signal that their contributions are shaping the direction of the conversation.

**I — Invest**
Show that you are genuinely interested in their perspective for its own sake, not only as material for counter-argument. Ask questions that go beyond what you need to know tactically.

**T — Test**
Once the previous four are in place, gently test the robustness of the rapport — introduce a mildly challenging question and observe whether the suspect remains engaged or becomes defensive. If they remain open, the rapport is real.

Use ORBIT as a diagnostic: if any element is missing, address it before moving to influence techniques.
