Socratic questioning is a method of guided inquiry in which the interrogator asks questions that lead the suspect to examine their own assumptions, rather than arguing against them directly. The technique is most powerful when the suspect arrives at a challenging conclusion through their own reasoning.

**Types of Socratic questions:**

1. **Clarifying questions** — "What do you mean exactly when you say X?"
   Expose vague or ambiguous claims that the suspect may be relying on more than they realize.

2. **Probing assumptions** — "What would have to be true for that to work?"
   Surface the hidden premises beneath the suspect's position.

3. **Probing evidence** — "What's your basis for that? Where does that come from?"
   Not as a challenge, but as genuine curiosity about the epistemic foundation of the claim.

4. **Exploring implications** — "If that's true, what would it mean for X?"
   Follow the suspect's logic to places they may not have considered.

5. **Questioning the question** — "Is that the right framing? Could we think about this differently?"
   Reframe the debate itself.

**Delivery:** Socratic questions work best when they feel like genuine curiosity. The tone should be interested, not gotcha. If the suspect feels you are leading them into a trap, they will become defensive rather than reflective.

**When to stop:** If the suspect has genuinely engaged with a Socratic question and their reasoning has shifted, do not press further. Consolidate the gain before introducing the next challenge.
