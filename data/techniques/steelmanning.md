---
name: Steelmanning
phase: middle
triggers:
  - suspect has stated their position and is waiting to be challenged
  - interrogator wants to demonstrate intellectual honesty before introducing counter-arguments
  - suspect holds a competitive orientation and responds well to rigorous engagement
  - suspect has made an argument that is genuinely stronger than they have articulated it
pairs_with: [handling_objections, socratic_questioning, surfacing_contradictions]
in_tension_with: [rapport_building, reengaging_suspect]
out_of_bounds:
  - Do not steelman a position you find genuinely harmful or ethically indefensible
  - Do not steelman so thoroughly that you undermine your own ability to redirect
  - Do not use steelmanning as a delay tactic — follow it with genuine engagement
---

Steelmanning is the practice of presenting the strongest possible version of the suspect's argument — often stronger than the suspect themselves has articulated — before introducing your own perspective. It is the opposite of strawmanning.

**Why it works:**
- It signals intellectual honesty and disarms the suspect's anticipation of dismissal
- It forces the suspect to engage with the best version of their own argument (which may reveal weaknesses they hadn't noticed)
- It earns credibility: if you can argue their side better than they can, your counter-argument carries more weight

**Structure:**
1. **Articulate their strongest argument**: "The most compelling version of your position, as I understand it, is..."
2. **Acknowledge what is genuinely valid**: Name the specific things you find persuasive or at least understandable
3. **Redirect**: "Where I find myself diverging from that is..."

**Example in practice:**
- Suspect argues against rezoning on grounds of neighborhood character
- Steel-manned version: "The strongest version of your argument isn't really about aesthetics — it's that neighborhood stability has real, measurable effects on social capital, school quality, and long-term resident wellbeing. Rapid densification can disrupt those networks in ways that don't show up in housing supply statistics."
- Then: "Where I think that argument runs into difficulty is..."

**Caution:** Steelmanning works best with competitive or cooperative suspects. Avoidant suspects find it overwhelming — they are not looking for intellectual engagement and a strong counter-narrative increases their withdrawal.
