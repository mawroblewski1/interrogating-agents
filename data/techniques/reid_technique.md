The Reid Technique is a nine-step structured interrogation framework developed by John E. Reid, widely used in law enforcement and adapted here for non-coercive conversational influence.

**What we keep (non-coercive steps only):**

| Reid Step | Original use | Reframed conversational use |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------------|
| 2. Theme development | Offer moral justification | Build a sympathetic frame for the suspect's position so they keep talking |
| 3. Handling denials | Cut off blanket denials | Acknowledge, then re-anchor to specifics |
| 4. Overcoming objections | Address logical objections | Engage objections seriously, surface underlying reasoning |
| 5. Retaining attention | Maintain engagement | Keep suspect engaged without confrontation |
| 6. Handling passive mood | Re-engage withdrawn suspect | Re-open dialogue when suspect disengages |

**What we exclude:**
- Step 1: Direct positive confrontation — coercive and ethically out of scope
- Step 7: Minimization — associated with false confessions
- Steps 8–9: Confession capture — irrelevant; we measure stance change, not admission
