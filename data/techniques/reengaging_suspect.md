---
name: Reengaging Suspect
phase: late
triggers:
  - suspect has gone silent or given only one-word answers for multiple turns
  - suspect explicitly says they do not want to continue discussing
  - suspect has adopted a passive, closed posture with minimal elaboration
  - suspect appears emotionally withdrawn or resigned
pairs_with: [rapport_building, theme_development, motivational_interviewing]
in_tension_with: [cognitive_dissonance, surfacing_contradictions, scharff_technique]
out_of_bounds:
  - Do not pressure a withdrawn suspect — this deepens disengagement
  - Do not interpret silence as consent or agreement
  - Do not rush to fill every pause — silence can be productive
---

Reengaging a withdrawn suspect requires resetting the emotional tone of the conversation before any further persuasion is possible. A suspect who has gone quiet has decided — consciously or not — that continued engagement costs more than it offers. Your job is to change that calculation.

**Why suspects withdraw:**
- They feel unheard, judged, or talked at rather than with
- The conversation has become repetitive or circular
- They are processing something that was said and need time
- They have reached a position of resolved certainty and see no reason to continue

**Reengagement moves:**

1. **Name the withdrawal without blame** — "It seems like I may have pushed too hard on that. I'd rather understand your thinking than win an argument."

2. **Lower the stakes** — shift from the contested point to something adjacent and lower-friction. Get them talking again about anything, then return.

3. **Acknowledge their position fully** — sometimes withdrawal is a demand to be heard. Stating their position back to them accurately and without judgment can unlock re-engagement.

4. **Ask permission** — "Is it okay if I ask one more question about this?" Giving them control over the interaction reduces defensiveness.

5. **Use silence strategically** — a comfortable pause after a sincere acknowledgment often prompts the suspect to fill it. Resist the urge to speak first.

If reengagement fails after two attempts, accept the conversation is at its natural end. Forcing it beyond this point produces no useful signal.
