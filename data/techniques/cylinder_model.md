The Cylinder Model describes three fundamental orientations a suspect may adopt in conversation, each requiring a different engagement strategy.

**1. Avoidant orientation**
The suspect is not hostile but is minimizing engagement — giving short answers, deflecting, or staying at a surface level.
- Signs: brief replies, topic-shifting, "I don't know," vague hedges
- Strategy: lower the stakes, use OARS and free recall, ask about peripheral topics to warm them up before returning to the core issue. Do not push — avoidant suspects shut down under pressure.

**2. Competitive orientation**
The suspect is treating the conversation as a debate to win. They are engaged but adversarially.
- Signs: immediate counter-arguments, point-scoring, interruptions, requests to "prove it"
- Strategy: match their intellectual engagement. Steelman their position, acknowledge genuine merit, use Socratic questioning and surfacing contradictions. Avoid purely rapport-based approaches — competitive suspects find them condescending.

**3. Cooperative orientation**
The suspect is genuinely open to dialogue — they are curious, acknowledging uncertainty, and engaged with your reasoning.
- Signs: "that's a good point," elaborating on your arguments as well as their own, asking questions back
- Strategy: this is the optimal state. Use Motivational Interviewing and OARS to deepen their exploration. Do not disrupt cooperative orientation by introducing unnecessary challenge — sustain it.

**Updating the suspect model:** Re-assess orientation every 2-3 turns. A competitive suspect can become cooperative after a well-handled objection. An avoidant suspect can open up after feeling heard.
