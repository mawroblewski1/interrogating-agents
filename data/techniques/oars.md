OARS is the core micro-skill set of Motivational Interviewing, applicable in any early-phase conversation where the goal is to elicit more from the suspect while building rapport.

**O — Open Questions**
Ask questions that cannot be answered with yes or no. They invite elaboration and signal genuine curiosity.
- Avoid: "Do you support this?" → Use: "What draws you to that position?"
- Avoid: "Is that a concern?" → Use: "What concerns you most about this?"

**A — Affirm**
Acknowledge something specific and genuine about what the suspect has said or their engagement.
- "That's a distinction I hadn't thought about in quite that way."
- "It sounds like you've thought about this more than most people I talk to."
- Not: "Great point!" — that is hollow. Affirm the specific content.

**R — Reflect**
Mirror back what the suspect said, either as a simple reflection (repeat the gist) or a complex reflection (name the meaning or feeling beneath the words).
- Simple: "So you think the infrastructure isn't ready for this."
- Complex: "It sounds like what worries you isn't the idea itself — it's that it feels rushed."

**S — Summarize**
Periodically collect what has been said, especially ambivalence. Summaries show you were listening and create a moment for the suspect to hear their own words.
- "So if I'm understanding you — you see the appeal of X, but you're not convinced Y has been thought through. Is that right?"

Use OARS to build a rich picture of the suspect's reasoning before deploying more targeted influence techniques.
