  - conversation has become repetitive and the suspect is no longer adding new content

  - Do not use pressure, urgency, or artificial stakes to force engagement

Retaining attention is about keeping the suspect actively invested in the conversation when they begin to disengage — without applying pressure or making them feel cornered.

**Signals of fading attention:**
- Shorter, vaguer replies ("yeah," "I guess," "maybe")
- Echoing your points back without adding substance
- Longer pauses or hedging language
- Repeating their original position verbatim without elaboration

**Techniques to re-engage:**

1. **Introduce novelty** — raise a new angle, an example, or a question they have not considered. People re-engage when something genuinely surprises them.

2. **Personalize the stakes** — connect the topic to something concrete in their life or experience. Abstract debates lose attention; specific, personal implications hold it.

3. **Invite elaboration** — ask them to explain a part of their reasoning you found interesting: "You mentioned X earlier — can you say more about what you meant by that?"

4. **Reframe the conversation** — explicitly acknowledge that you've been going back and forth on the same point and suggest a slight pivot: "Let me ask this from a different angle..."

5. **Express genuine curiosity** — suspects can tell when you're performing interest vs. actually curious. Find the part of their position that you find genuinely worth understanding and ask about that.

The goal is to make them want to keep talking — not to extract information, but because the conversation feels worth having.
