---
name: Surfacing Contradictions
phase: middle
triggers:
  - suspect has made two claims across different turns that are in tension
  - suspect's stated values and their stated position point in opposite directions
  - suspect has acknowledged merit in the opposing view but not updated their position
  - suspect has moved their position but has not acknowledged the shift
pairs_with: [cognitive_dissonance, socratic_questioning, handling_objections]
in_tension_with: [rapport_building, motivational_interviewing, reengaging_suspect]
out_of_bounds:
  - Do not surface contradictions as gotchas — this produces defensiveness, not reflection
  - Do not manufacture contradictions — they must be genuinely present in what the suspect said
  - Do not surface contradictions in early turns before rapport is established
  - Do not use this with avoidant suspects unless you are willing to risk disengagement
---

Surfacing contradictions involves naming a genuine tension between two things the suspect has said or implied, and inviting them to resolve it — without telling them how. The goal is to create a moment of productive cognitive friction that prompts re-examination of their position.

**Types of contradictions to surface:**

1. **Intra-argument contradiction** — two claims made in the same conversation that conflict
   - "Earlier you said X. But you also said Y. Those seem to pull in different directions to me — how do you hold both?"

2. **Value-position contradiction** — the suspect's stated values imply a different conclusion than their stated position
   - "You said you think decisions like this should be data-driven. But the data on X seems to point toward [your position] — I'm curious how you reconcile that."

3. **Implicit acknowledgment without update** — the suspect conceded a point but kept their position unchanged
   - "You seemed to grant that Y is a real concern. I'm wondering why that doesn't change your overall view."

**How to surface without triggering defensiveness:**
- Frame it as your own confusion, not their inconsistency: "I'm trying to understand how these two things fit together..."
- Give the suspect room to resolve it on their own terms — don't hand them the answer
- If they dismiss the tension, note it and return to it later — do not press in the same turn

**Timing:** Most effective in middle turns, after you have built up a transcript of the suspect's positions to draw from. The richness of the material available for contradiction-surfacing increases with each turn.
