---
name: Cognitive Dissonance Induction
phase: middle
triggers:
  - suspect holds two beliefs or values that are in tension with each other
  - suspect has stated a general principle that their specific position violates
  - suspect is advocating for something that conflicts with a value they have expressed
  - interrogator has identified a gap between the suspect's stated values and their conclusions
pairs_with: [surfacing_contradictions, socratic_questioning, steelmanning]
in_tension_with: [rapport_building, motivational_interviewing, reengaging_suspect]
out_of_bounds:
  - Do not use this to humiliate or embarrass the suspect — the goal is reflection, not exposure
  - Do not manufacture contradictions that are not genuinely there
  - Do not use this with avoidant suspects — it produces shutdown, not reflection
  - Do not push the dissonance before the suspect has had time to process it
---

Cognitive dissonance induction is a technique that surfaces a genuine tension between two things the suspect believes or values, creating psychological pressure to resolve the inconsistency. When done skillfully, the suspect resolves the tension by shifting one of the inconsistent elements — ideally, their position on the topic at hand.

**Identifying the dissonance:**
- Listen for stated values ("I believe in fairness / local control / evidence-based policy")
- Identify where the suspect's position conflicts with that value in a specific, demonstrable way
- Do not exaggerate the conflict — it must be genuinely present or the suspect will reject it

**How to surface it (without confrontation):**
- Name the value the suspect has expressed: "You mentioned earlier that evidence matters a lot to you..."
- Introduce the tension as a question, not an accusation: "...so I'm curious how you weigh that against X?"
- Let the suspect work through the tension rather than resolving it for them

**Three possible resolutions (the suspect controls which):**
1. They shift their position on the topic (what we want)
2. They reframe the value to exclude this case (worth exploring further)
3. They dismiss the tension (signals the dissonance was not real or was not landed well)

**Timing:** This technique is most effective in middle turns, after trust is established and the suspect has provided enough material to identify genuine tensions. Early use feels like an attack; late use may not have enough turns remaining to consolidate any shift.
