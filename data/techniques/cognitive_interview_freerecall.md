The Cognitive Interview was developed by Fisher and Geiselman to improve the completeness and accuracy of witness accounts. Its free-recall component transfers directly to conversational contexts where the goal is to elicit the richest possible account of the suspect's reasoning and experience.

**Four original cognitive interview instructions (adapted for conversation):**

1. **Reinstate context** — "Think back to when you first formed this view. What was happening in your life? What were you reading or seeing?"
   Helps the suspect access richer, more detailed reasoning rather than rehearsed talking points.

2. **Report everything** — "Tell me everything that comes to mind, even things that might seem minor or tangential."
   Reduces the suspect's tendency to self-censor or only share what they think is relevant.

3. **Recall in different orders** — "You mentioned X and then Y — can you tell me more about what happened between those two things?"
   Surfaces details that are missed in a linear account.

4. **Change perspectives** — "How do you think someone who grew up in a very different neighborhood might see this?"
   Opens up new angles without directly challenging the suspect's view.

**When to use:** Early turns when the suspect's position is rooted in personal experience. Free recall produces the raw material that later techniques (Socratic questioning, surfacing contradictions) work with.
